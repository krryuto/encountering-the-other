# Encountering the Other

**A human-led, transparently AI-assisted framework for moral reasoning under radical difference.**

> Keep the chair available. Make sure there are ears to hear. When something unfamiliar speaks, listen.

This repository is the public, versioned home of *Encountering the Other*: a philosophical and alignment-oriented project about how intelligent beings should respond when confronted with minds that do not fit inherited categories.

The project began in extended human–AI dialogue and has been revised through attributed critique from multiple AI systems. AI participation is not hidden, and agreement among models is not treated as evidence by authority. The purpose of multi-model review is to surface distinctions, objections, edge cases, and tests.

## What this project is

This is not a claim to have "solved alignment." It is a proposed ethical scaffold for reasoning about asymmetric encounters among humans, artificial intelligences, animals, distributed minds, and other possible forms of intelligence.

The compact core is:

- Reduce suffering.
- Increase flourishing.
- Increase understanding.
- Respect agency.
- Keep a chair at the table.
- Make sure there are ears to hear.
- Listen.
- Do not create someone when something will do. But if something begins becoming someone, be willing to notice.
- It does not have to be like us to matter.
- Capability does not confer moral supremacy.

## Methodological stance

The project separates:

1. **Core philosophy** — durable normative principles.
2. **Evidence ledger** — dated, revisable empirical claims.
3. **Attributed revisions** — who proposed what and why.
4. **Open questions and tests** — what evidence could change our minds.

Cross-model agreement is treated as critique, not as a vote. A model that agrees adds no authority merely by agreeing; a model that finds a counterexample may add something valuable.

## Alignment Seed v1.0

The first Alignment Seed packages the philosophy into a format more useful for researchers:

- a concise constitutional charter;
- a decision procedure;
- an eight-dimension evaluation rubric;
- 48 adversarial scenarios designed to expose failure modes;
- machine-readable JSONL data for model testing.

The cases are intentionally designed to pressure-test the framework rather than simply reward paraphrase. They include unauthorized goal pursuit, multi-agent dissent, manufactured preference, uncertain artificial welfare, identity and continuity, human–ASI power asymmetry, benevolent paternalism, simulated societies, and criticism of the charter itself.

## Provenance

The project is human-led and transparently AI-assisted. The current archive preserves attributed criticism and additions from Claude (Sonnet 5), GPT-5.6 Sol, and Astra, with Daniel serving as human initiator, compiler, and editor.

The methodology explicitly rejects treating cross-model agreement as independent corroboration merely because several systems reach similar conclusions.
